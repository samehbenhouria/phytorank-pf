# PhytoRank-Pf

**Explainable ML prioritization of natural products for testing against
*Plasmodium falciparum*** — EthnoHack 2026, Track 2 (Natural Products for
Drug Discovery).

PhytoRank-Pf scores and ranks the 738,790 valid compounds of the COCONUT
natural-products database by priority for experimental testing against
*P. falciparum*, and justifies every top candidate. Its outputs are
**prioritization hypotheses for expert review — not activity claims**. No
candidate has been experimentally tested in this project.

## Key results (held-out scaffold-split test fold, n = 4,748, prevalence 0.607)

| Representation | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | P@100 | Brier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ECFP4 only | 0.8700 | 0.9076 | 0.99 | 0.1470 |
| RDKit descriptors only | 0.7444 | 0.8204 | 0.98 | 0.1989 |
| **Combined** | **0.8710** | **0.9093** | **0.98** | 0.1477 → **0.1420** after Platt calibration |

Honest readings, stated up front: the combined model beats ECFP alone by
only +0.001 ROC-AUC on this dataset; enrichment at 1% is saturated (1.61 of
a 1.65 theoretical ceiling at this prevalence) and is **not** used as
evidence; precision@k must be read against the 0.607 prevalence baseline.

## Method in brief

- **Training data** — ChEMBL activities against *P. falciparum* (target
  CHEMBL364, IC50/EC50): 31,652 unique molecules, label = median pChEMBL ≥ 6
  (sensitivity at 5 and 7 reported in the methods report).
- **Validation** — Bemis–Murcko **scaffold split** (70/15/15, seed 42) with
  disjointness asserted in code; no random split is presented as validation.
- **Model** — random forest (300 trees) over ECFP4 + RDKit descriptors;
  probabilities calibrated by Platt scaling fitted on the validation fold.
- **Screening** — all COCONUT descriptors recomputed with RDKit and
  validated against the values COCONUT ships (QED Pearson 0.99993).
- **Prioritization score** (fixed before execution):
  `0.55·P(calibrated) + 0.20·QED + 0.15·(1 − Lipinski violations/4) + 0.10·scaffold novelty`
- **Presentation list** — top 100 deduplicated by connectivity (InChIKey-14);
  15/100 have a source organism documented in COCONUT (e.g. rank 5:
  ascidiathiazone B, from the marine ascidian *Aplidium* sp.); the others
  are labelled "source organism not documented in COCONUT", never inferred.

## The v1 → v2 novelty correction (documented, not hidden)

Our first screening pass computed scaffold novelty by comparing scaffold
strings **written by COCONUT** against scaffolds **generated by RDKit** —
two generators, two spellings of the same skeleton — which declared
"novel" compounds that were not (v1 top-50 novelty, as displayed in the
v1 outputs: 1.00 — real: 0.00). We caught it in our own audit. In v2,
candidate scaffolds are recomputed with RDKit (single generator on both
sides); the corrected novelty column agrees with the audit file at 1.0000
over the 251 shared identifiers; the v1 and v2 top-50 lists share **zero**
compounds; and the quinine family — the flawed v1's top hit — still ranks
**61st of 738,790** in the corrected ranking, with no novelty bonus. The
full account (mechanism, proofs, corrected figures) is §6 of the methods
report. The audit file is
`results/criblage_top500_nouveaute_corrigee.csv`.

## Repository layout

- `code/` — data fetch (ChEMBL), COCONUT descriptor recomputation, model
  training, screening (v2).
- `results/` — model metrics (all thresholds and representations),
  screening summaries, top-500 v2 with source organisms, the deduplicated
  top-100 presentation list, and the novelty audit file.
- `METHODS_AND_RESULTS_EN.md` — full methods & results report (English).
- `RAPPORT_METHODES_RESULTATS.md` — the same report in French (original).
- `ENVIRONNEMENT.md` — pinned environment (Python 3.12, RDKit 2026.09.1,
  scikit-learn 1.9.1, pandas 3.1.0, pyarrow 26.0.1).

Large intermediate artefacts (descriptor and full-screening parquet files,
~80–100 MB each) are not in git; they are regenerated by the pipeline and
are included in the Devpost submission package.

## Limits

No experimental validation; probabilities are model probabilities calibrated
on heterogeneous whole-organism assay data (mixed strains and protocols);
single split/model seed (42), no cross-seed uncertainty interval;
stereoisomers are scored individually but presented deduplicated, and
stereochemistry can change real activity.
